## 1. Narrative summary

The presentation tells one continuous story in which the customer — an Indian
industrial or commercial business — is the hero, and WattZap is the guide. It
opens not with WattZap's history but with the customer's monthly reality: an
electricity bill whose amount is perfectly visible while its causes are not.
Consumption, maximum demand, power factor, tariff category, time-of-use
behaviour, billing accuracy and solar performance all shape the final number,
yet the owner can rarely say which part of the bill was avoidable.

The tension deepens on the third slide: the market the customer must navigate
is fragmented. Solar EPCs, electrical contractors, meter vendors, APFC
suppliers, software companies, auditors, maintenance providers and financiers
each solve a component — but nobody owns the complete outcome, and the
customer is left to integrate the pieces and answer the unanswerable question:
*what actually reduced my bill, and by how much?*

WattZap enters as the structured, accountable alternative. Its operating logic
is deliberately un-vendor-like: understand the baseline first, identify the
cost drivers, prioritise high-confidence actions, implement only what is
needed, measure the actual result, and expand only after value is
demonstrated. The intervention follows the evidence — never a predetermined
product.

That logic unfolds as the six-stage journey which the audience should leave
remembering: **Diagnose → Protect → Optimise → Generate → Finance → Prove.**
One slide per stage shows what the customer gets: EnergyScan turns assumptions
into measurable opportunities; the four Guards stop losses that should never
have been occurring; EMS makes monitoring lead to better operational
decisions; SolarFit sizes generation to the site's energy reality rather than
its roof; a range of commercial structures — from customer-funded to
savings-linked — removes the capital barrier where the case supports it; and
Savings Assurance makes the maths visible: agreed baseline, minus adjusted
actual, equals verified savings.

Two closing moves ground the story. A modular data-architecture slide shows
that the machinery behind all this starts with the data a customer already has
— bills and existing meters — and grows only where extra visibility creates
value. Then the focus slide states plainly where WattZap is starting (rice
mills and energy-intensive SMEs, where savings are material, measurable and
repeatable), where it can expand, and how an engagement actually begins:
share bills, run an EnergyScan, pilot one high-confidence opportunity,
implement, measure, expand.

The deck closes on the positioning line it opened with — *measured savings,
not equipment sales* — and a single, low-friction call to action: start with
one site and one EnergyScan. No claims are made that cannot be evidenced;
where proof points will exist later, explicit placeholders mark the spot.

### Slide 1 — Opening

- **Headline:** Industrial electricity costs, turned into measured savings
- **Subheadline:** We help businesses diagnose, protect, optimise, generate, finance and prove energy savings
- **On-slide text:** Kicker: "Measured savings, not equipment sales" ·
  Supporting: "WattZap Energy Solutions is an industrial energy-savings and
  optimisation company for Indian industrial and commercial customers —
  starting with rice mills and energy-intensive SMEs." · Chips: "Industrial
  and commercial focus" / "One structured end-to-end journey" / "Outcomes
  measured, not promised"
- **Arrangement:** Title-slide master — kicker, headline with gradient accent
  on "measured savings", subtitle, one supporting sentence, three chips.
- **Visual:** Official WattZap icon as the controlled decorative element
  (built into the master template).
- **Speaker notes:** Establish the category in one breath: industrial
  energy savings, measured. Do not explain products, history or the founder.
  Land the positioning line and pause — it is the thesis of the next 13
  slides. If the audience remembers one phrase, it must be this one.
- **Transition:** "To see why 'measured' matters, start with the one document
  every factory already gets — the electricity bill."

### Slide 2 — The bill is only the visible symptom

- **Headline:** The bill shows the cost — not what causes it
- **Subheadline:** One visible number, shaped by many hidden factors
- **On-slide text:** Three columns — "What the bill shows": The final amount,
  units consumed and the due date / Penalties and charges, buried in line
  items / A number that arrives after the money is spent. "What stays
  hidden": Demand, power factor and time-of-use behaviour / Tariff category,
  contract demand and billing accuracy / Machine loading, schedules and solar
  performance. "What owners cannot see": Which part of the bill was avoidable
  / Whether the cause is operational, technical or contractual / Whether
  promised savings actually materialised. Chip: "Customers know what they
  paid — not what they could have prevented".
- **Arrangement:** Three-column overview, reading left to right from visible
  → hidden → consequence.
- **Visual:** The column progression itself is the "bill breaking into hidden
  drivers" concept, rendered in the locked card language.
- **Speaker notes:** Name two or three factors conversationally (power
  factor, maximum demand, time-of-use) — do not read all nine. Ask the
  audience: "when your bill jumps 18% in a month, how do you find out why?"
  The point is asymmetry: the cost is visible, the cause is not.
- **Transition:** "So the obvious move is to call a vendor. That's where the
  second problem starts."

### Slide 3 — The current market is fragmented

- **Subheadline:** Each provider solves a part; the customer must connect the pieces

- **Arrangement:** 2×2 card grid — the four vendor families surrounding the
  customer's problem; the unanswered question anchors the slide as the note.
- **Visual:** The four-card grid stands in for the "customer surrounded by

- **Speaker notes:** Be respectful — many of these providers do good

  frame WattZap as the party that makes their component finally provable.
  End by reading the customer question aloud — it is the hinge of the deck.
- **Transition:** "WattZap was built to own that question. And it starts in a
  different place than a vendor would."

### Slide 4 — WattZap begins differently

- **Headline:** WattZap begins with the opportunity, not a product

### Slide 5 — The complete WattZap journey

- **Headline:** One journey from diagnosis to proven savings

### Slide 6 — Diagnose before investing

- **Headline:** Every engagement begins with evidence
- **Subheadline:** EnergyScan separates assumptions from measurable opportunities
- **On-slide text:** Two options — "EnergyScan Lite — A first screening from
  existing bills": Initial bill screening and qualification / High-level
  identification of opportunity areas / Preliminary savings areas, at no
  obligation. "EnergyScan Pro — A detailed diagnosis of the whole site"
  (badge: The foundation): Detailed bill, tariff and interval-data analysis /
  Site, equipment and load-profile assessment / An agreed baseline for future
  verification / A prioritised implementation roadmap. Note: "The output is a
  prioritised action plan — not an audit report that sits on a shelf."
- **Arrangement:** Two-option comparison; Pro is visually featured as the
  foundation of everything downstream.
- **Visual:** Side-by-side Lite/Pro cards.
- **Speaker notes:** Two things distinguish this from a traditional audit:
  the output is a prioritised, implementable roadmap; and the baseline agreed
  here is the same baseline savings are verified against later — the audit
  and the proof are one system. Lite is the no-friction entry: one bill.
- **Transition:** "The first items on that roadmap are usually not new
  equipment at all — they're leaks."

### Slide 7 — Protect against recurring leakage

- **Headline:** The fastest savings stop losses already occurring
- **Subheadline:** WattZap Guard — one protection layer around the electricity bill
- **On-slide text:** Four cards — "PF Guard": Identifies power-factor
  penalties and their causes / Reduces reactive-power exposure / Verifies
  that penalties actually fall. "Demand Guard": Tracks demand against
  sanctioned limits / Flags spike patterns and what they cost / Controls
  demand charges before they recur. "Bill Guard": Checks every bill for
  anomalies and errors / Flags tariff issues and inconsistencies / Supports
  recovery where money is owed. "Solar Guard": Verifies solar delivers what
  was promised / Flags underperformance and downtime / Values lost generation
  in rupees. Note: "Protection comes before expansion — the four Guards work
  as one layer, not separate products."
- **Arrangement:** 2×2 grid, one Guard per card, unified by the note.
- **Visual:** The four Guards presented as one shield around the bill.
- **Speaker notes:** These savings need no capital and no behaviour change —
  they stop money that should never have left. Pick one Guard and tell it as
  a story (e.g. a failed APFC panel that nobody notices until the penalty
  appears). Keep the four framed as one protection layer with four lenses.
- **Transition:** "Once the leaks are stopped, the question becomes: how well
  is the site actually being run?"

### Slide 8 — Optimise before adding more infrastructure

- **Headline:** Use the existing site more intelligently first
- **Subheadline:** EMS Lite and EMS Pro — monitoring that leads to better decisions
- **On-slide text:** Five steps — See: Site and machine-level visibility ·

  Semi-automated and automated control. Chips: "Monitoring is only valuable

- **Arrangement:** Five-step process flow: See → Understand → Decide → Act →

- **Visual:** The maturity flow itself; no dashboard screenshots, no
  architecture detail.
- **Speaker notes:** Differentiate from dashboard-only software in one line:
  a chart never saved anyone money — a rescheduled dryer run does. EMS Lite
  starts the ladder; EMS Pro deepens it towards automation. Each step is
  optional and evidence-gated.
- **Transition:** "Only after protection and optimisation does WattZap talk
  about generating power — and even then, in a specific order."

### Slide 9 — Add the right generation and storage

- **Headline:** Solar sized to your energy reality, not your roof
- **Subheadline:** Generation and storage follow the load — never the other way round
- **On-slide text:** Three columns — "Sized to the site": Daytime load,

  right-sized, evidence-based design / Owned, leased or PPA-structured solar

- **Subheadline:** A valuable project should not fail only because of upfront capital
- **On-slide text:** Three options — "Customer-funded — Full ownership where

- **Visual:** Side-by-side structure cards with the qualifier as the note.
- **Speaker notes:** Read the qualifier deliberately — it is a credibility
  feature, not a hedge. The reason WattZap can even discuss savings-linked

  measure. With financiers, this slide plus slide 11 is the core pitch.
- **Transition:** "Every one of those structures depends on the same
  question: can the savings be proved?"

### Slide 11 — Prove where the savings came from

- **Headline:** Savings are credible when the maths is visible
- **Subheadline:** Agreed baseline − adjusted actual cost = verified savings
- **On-slide text:** Three columns — "Agreed baseline": The 'before' cost,
  agreed before changes / Built from bills and measured site data / The
  reference every result is checked against. "Adjusted actual": Measured cost
  after implementation / Agreed adjustments: production, hours, tariffs /
  Seasonality and extraordinary events included. "Verified savings":
  Attributed by source: penalties, billing, operations, solar / Monthly
  reports for customers and financiers / Tracked continuously, not claimed
  once. Chips: "WattZap's role continues after implementation" / "[Insert

- **Speaker notes:** This is the differentiator slide — slow down. Explain

  flattered or cheated by output changes. Savings are attributed by source,
  so every rupee has an explanation. The placeholder chip is deliberate:
  swap in the first verified pilot figure when it exists, never before.
- **Transition:** "All of this runs on data — so here is the machinery, and

## 3. Opening script (Slides 1–3, 60–90 seconds)

> "Thank you for the time. WattZap is an industrial energy-savings company —
> our whole positioning is in one line: measured savings, not equipment
> sales. Here's the problem we work on. Every factory gets an electricity
> bill, and the amount is perfectly visible. What's not visible is the cause:
> demand charges, power factor, time-of-use patterns, tariff issues, billing
> errors, solar that quietly underperforms. Most owners can tell you what
> they paid last month — almost none can tell you what part of it was
> avoidable. And when they try to fix it, they meet a fragmented market: a
> solar EPC sells panels, a contractor sells equipment, a software company
> sells a dashboard, an auditor sells a report, a financier sells capital.
> Every one of them solves a piece — and nobody owns the final question:
> what actually reduced my bill, and by how much? WattZap exists to own that
> question, end to end. Let me show you how."

## 4. Closing script

> "So to bring it back to one line: measured savings, not equipment sales.
> We're not asking anyone to commit to a journey today. The way this starts
> is deliberately small: share a few recent electricity bills, we run an
> EnergyScan, and we come back with evidence — where the money is going and
> which single opportunity we'd pilot first. We implement that one thing,
> measure the result against an agreed baseline, and only then talk about
> what to unlock next. If the savings aren't there, the numbers will say so
> — that's the point of how we work. So the practical next step: one site,
> one EnergyScan. Can we pick the site before we leave?"

## 5. Audience adaptations

Same slides, different verbal emphasis — never redesign per audience.

- **Industrial customer:** Slow down on slides 2, 6 and 7 — their bill,
  their first free step, their fastest savings. Make slide 13's six steps
  feel like a concrete offer ("bills this week, findings next week"). Skim
  slide 12; mention slide 10 only as "there are ways to do this without
  capex".
- **Technology partner (IoT, metering, software):** Slides 3, 5 and 12 carry
  the meeting. On slide 3, position them as a component provider whose work
  finally becomes provable inside a system; on slide 12, point at the layer
  where they plug in (metering, connectivity, sub-metering, control). Keep
  slides 6–10 brisk — they need the system, not the sales pitch.
- **Financing partner (bank, NBFC):** Slides 10 and 11 are the pitch. The
  message: WattZap creates measurable, attributable cash flows and the
  reporting that lets a financier underwrite them — savings-linked
  structures only exist because the measurement layer exists. Be explicit
  about the placeholder on slide 11: verified pilot data will replace it,
  and they will receive the same monthly verification reports.
- **Investor or strategic advisor:** Emphasise the arc rather than any
  stage: the fragmentation gap (slide 3), the progressive unlock model that
  compounds customer value (slides 4–5), verification as the moat and the
  enabler of savings-linked economics (slide 11), and disciplined sequencing
  via sector playbooks (slide 13). Acknowledge openly that proof points are
  placeholders at this stage — the deck is built to receive them without
  restructuring.

## 6. Final quality check

- Audience understands WattZap within three slides — yes: category and
  positioning land on slide 1; slides 2–3 define the problem WattZap owns.
- Begins with the customer problem, not company history — yes: no founder
  bio, incorporation detail or mission prose anywhere.
- Not presentable as a generic solar EPC — yes: solar appears only at stage
  4 of 6, explicitly sized to load, with "value over installed capacity" as
  the stated goal.
- Journey is memorable — yes: stated on slide 1, mapped on slide 5, then one
  slide per stage in order; slide 13 replays it as an engagement path.
- Products feel like one system — yes: every product is introduced inside
  its journey stage; Guards are framed as one layer; EMS as one ladder.
- Measurement and verification as major differentiators — yes: "measured" is
  in the headline of slide 1, the baseline is planted on slide 6, and slide
  11 is wholly devoted to the verification equation.
- No unsupported facts — yes: zero numbers, customer names, capacity or
  partnership claims; two explicit placeholders (slide 11 chip, slide 14
  contact line) and qualified language on financing ("no model is promised
  universally").
- Concise enough to present — yes: every slide passes the brand density
  limits; depth lives in these speaker notes.
- Works across customers, partners, financiers, advisors — yes: see
  adaptations above; no slide is audience-exclusive.
- Clear, practical closing action — yes: one site, one EnergyScan, with the
  six-step engagement path shown before the close.

**Intended takeaway:** WattZap gives industrial customers a structured and
accountable way to understand their electricity costs, stop avoidable
losses, improve operations, invest intelligently and continuously verify the
savings achieved.
